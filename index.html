Overview

Seven Optimizers, One Framework

This project connects classical numerical analysis theory to modern deep learning practice by benchmarking gradient-based optimizers across a range of loss landscapes — from smooth strongly-convex quadratics to ill-conditioned Rosenbrock functions and neural network training tasks.

📉
First-order · Baseline
Gradient Descent (GD)
Fixed step size; O(1/k) convergence on smooth convex functions. Sensitive to learning rate choice and conditioning.
🎲
First-order · Stochastic
Stochastic GD (SGD)
Mini-batch gradient estimates enable online learning and implicit regularization. Noisy updates aid escaping local minima.
🚀
First-order · Memory
Momentum (Heavy Ball)
Exponential moving average of gradients dampens oscillations in narrow valleys, speeding convergence on ill-conditioned problems.
⚡
Accelerated · Lookahead
Nesterov AGD
Evaluates gradient at a lookahead point. Achieves optimal O(1/k²) rate on strongly convex functions — confirmed theoretically and empirically.
🎯
Adaptive · Per-parameter
AdaGrad
Accumulates squared gradients to adapt learning rates per parameter. Excels on sparse features; suffers from monotone lr decay on dense problems.
🔄
Adaptive · EMA
RMSProp
Replaces AdaGrad's cumulative sum with exponential moving average, preventing lr from vanishing. Well-suited for non-stationary objectives.
👑
Adaptive + Momentum
Adam
Combines RMSProp and momentum with bias correction. Fast convergence in practice; may generalise worse than SGD on smooth convex problems.

Convergence Rates

Theoretical Guarantees

Optimizer Convergence Rate Adaptive LR Best For Limitation
Gradient Descent O(1/k) No Smooth convex, baseline Slow on ill-conditioned
SGD O(1/√k) No Large-scale, deep learning High variance, tuning-heavy
Momentum O(1/k) improved No Ill-conditioned problems Overshoot in sharp minima
Nesterov AGD O(1/k²) No Strongly convex, optimal Sensitive to lr tuning
AdaGrad O(log k/√k) Yes Sparse gradients / NLP lr → 0 on dense problems
RMSProp O(1/√k) Yes RNNs, non-stationary No bias correction
Adam O(1/√k) Yes General deep learning May not generalise as SGD

Research Questions

What We Investigated

01
How do step size (learning rate) and curvature of the loss landscape affect convergence rate and stability?
02
When does an adaptive learning rate help versus hurt generalisation on smooth convex and non-convex problems?
03
What is the relationship between Lipschitz smoothness, strong convexity, and convergence guarantees?
04
How do momentum-based methods compare to classical accelerated gradient methods (Nesterov) in practice?
05
Do learning rate schedules (cosine decay, step decay, warmup) consistently improve over fixed learning rates across all optimizers?

Key Findings

Empirical Results

Momentum
2–4× Faster than Vanilla GD
SGD + Momentum converges 2–4× faster than vanilla GD on ill-conditioned problems, consistently across tested loss landscapes.
Adam vs SGD
Speed vs. Generalisation Trade-off
Adam reaches lower training loss faster but can generalise worse than SGD on smooth convex problems — consistent with the "sharp minima" hypothesis.
Nesterov AGD
Optimal O(1/k²) Rate Confirmed
Nesterov AGD achieves the theoretically optimal O(1/k²) convergence rate on strongly convex functions, matching theory with empirical evidence.
AdaGrad
Sparse vs Dense Gradient Tradeoff
AdaGrad excels on sparse gradient landscapes but suffers from monotone learning rate decay on dense problems, causing stagnation.
LR Schedules
Cosine Decay Consistently Wins
Learning rate schedules (cosine, step decay) consistently outperform fixed learning rates across all seven methods tested.
Condition Number
Key Predictor of Optimizer Choice
The condition number of the Hessian strongly predicts when adaptive methods outperform fixed-rate methods on a given problem.
